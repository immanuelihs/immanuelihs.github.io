§01 · Specialization

What I'm good at, and why it fits together.

01 — Agentic verification & faithfulness

I build both halves of making an AI agent trustworthy: catching what it gets wrong, and catching what it silently leaves out. ProvenanceGuard checks whether an agent's claims are actually grounded in the right source (+47% grounding accuracy). Lacuna checks the side most safety tooling skips entirely — omission, the fact that got dropped rather than fabricated (omission F1 0.603→0.747, beating an LLM-judge baseline at ~20× smaller). TraceForge then hardens the agent's behavior itself into replayable, auditable workflows (73% of production traffic served from frozen DAGs). My latest work asks a harder question than "did it omit something" — it attributes exactly which layer of an air-gapped pipeline the omission came from, and finds most of it isn't the model's fault at all (layer-attribution paper).

see Production systems ↓

02 — Domain-specific agents & vision-LLMs

General-purpose vision-language models don't read P&ID diagrams or CAD assemblies reliably out of the box. I continually pretrain and QLoRA-tune hybrid ViT backbones until they do, then wrap them in agents that reason over the result — one such system extracts from 300K+ engineering documents in production, another gives designers structured QC feedback and a 40% productivity gain. The same approach extends to medical report generation.

Patents: Hybrid Vision Encoder for Vision LLMs · Learnable Vision LLMs for Engineering Drawings · Continual Pretraining for Mechanical-Domain LLMs · Medical Report Generation with LLMs.

see Patents ↓

03 — Model compression & efficient inference

Trustworthy is only useful if it's also small and fast enough to run in the room where the decision gets made — often air-gapped, often on a Jetson, not an H200 cluster. I've patented multi-criteria structured pruning that gets 70% compression under 1% accuracy loss, generalized it into a compression platform used across LLMs and CNNs, and built training-free self-speculative decoding that gets lossless 1.34–1.67× latency reduction. It's the same instinct that let Lacuna's detector run at ~20× smaller than an LLM judge — compression and verification aren't separate skills for me, they're the same problem from two sides.

Patents: US20250272561 · US20240428073 · US20250045584 · US20250005362 · US20250272217 (the verification framework — patent US20250272217 — bridges directly back to Pillar 1).

04 — Where it started: classical CV & CNNs

Before vision-language models were the default tool, I was building and publishing plain CNNs that had to work: wavelet-based diagnosis from COVID-19 chest imaging, automotive brake-part defect detection, plant-disease classification from leaf images. The throughline from there to hybrid ViT vision-LLMs today hasn't changed — make a model see something specific, and trust its answer.

05 — Ph.D.: a fifth axis

My Ph.D. runs a fifth thread in parallel, deliberately in a different domain: causal, uncertainty-aware state-space models for irregular clinical time series — same rigor, different room. Under peer review; details are intentionally kept high-level until that's resolved.

[A] Agent reliability & factuality — deployed

Three shipped systems (see Specialization §01 ↑ for how they connect).

ProvenanceGuard

Multiverse Computing · deployed · 2025–26

First source-aware factuality verifier for MCP-based agents — a fine-tuned Router+NLI verifier that catches subtle hallucination and cross-source conflation in tool calls.

→ +47% grounding accuracy in accuracy-sensitive domains

arXiv:2606.18037

Lacuna

Multiverse Computing · deployed · 2025–26

First runtime omission-detection gate for tool-using agents — closes the recall-side blind spot that hallucination checkers structurally miss, in law- and medicine-domain agents. A fine-tuned atomic-entailment scorer beats an LLM-judge baseline at roughly 20× smaller model size.

→ omission F1 0.603 → 0.747

→ F1 0.747 vs. 0.618 (LLM-judge baseline), ~20× smaller model

ProvenanceGuard and Lacuna are both now being deployed for an enterprise technology partner.

Where Facts Go Missing (new)

Independent research · arXiv preprint · 2026

A direct continuation of the Lacuna/TraceForge line, one layer deeper: a nine-layer taxonomy (L0–L8) of where information omissions actually originate inside quantized, air-gapped LLM agent pipelines, with a per-layer attribution method (ablation + logit decomposition) and a runtime-detection framework built on top of it.

→ 75,476-trial analysis, 0.62 omission rate

→ 68% of omissions originate in deterministic middleware layers — not the model itself

arXiv:2607.22448

TraceForge

Multiverse Computing · production · patent filed, EP 26382950.9

Human-gated framework that hardens stochastic agent behavior into immutable workflow DAGs for regulated industries.

→ 73% of production requests served from frozen DAGs

→ −38% redundant executions
