WAVE 01 · 2018–2022

CNNs, deployed where they couldn't fail

My first company made reading devices for blind students in India. The constraints were brutal and clarifying: no internet, cheap edge hardware, users who couldn't work around a failure. We got CNNs running offline on devices that ended up in the hands of seven thousand people — and that experience permanently set my bar for “done”: a model counts when it works in the room where it's needed, not when the demo works.

WAVE 02 · 2022–2024

Domain-specific vision-LLMs, before they were standard practice

When vision-language models were still a research curiosity, I was betting they could learn to read the hardest documents in industry — CAD assemblies, P&ID diagrams, electrical drawings. We continually pretrained hybrid ViT backbones until they genuinely understood engineering conventions, then put them to work in production for Fortune 500 manufacturers. Four patents and an internal award later, that bet looks obvious. It wasn't at the time.

THE CONSTANT · 2022–now

The efficiency thread that runs through everything

Running through everything is one obsession: the model has to fit the hardware in the room — often a Jetson, sometimes air-gapped, never a datacenter. That produced five published US patents on structured pruning and model verification, and more recently training-free speculative decoding that speeds up LLMs without changing a single output token. Compression and verification look like different fields; done right, they're the same discipline — knowing exactly what a model can afford to lose.

WAVE 03 · 2024–now

Agent verification & faithfulness — the current frontier

Today I work on the question that decides whether agents get deployed at all: can you trust what they say? I've built both halves of the answer. ProvenanceGuard checks that an agent's claims trace to the right sources; Lacuna catches what agents silently leave out — the failure hallucination-checkers structurally can't see. TraceForge freezes validated agent behavior into replayable workflows for regulated industries. My latest research traces omissions layer by layer through the pipeline — and finds most of them aren't the model's fault. This work runs in production with an enterprise technology partner and a European national government, and I'm presenting it at UC Berkeley's Agentic AI Summit.
